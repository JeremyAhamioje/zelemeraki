It's one of the most common questions we're asked: is this building worth saving, or should we start again? There's no universal answer — but there are five questions that almost always settle it.

1. Is the structure sound?

Everything else depends on this. Cracks, damp, sagging slabs and corroded reinforcement can be minor or serious, and you can't tell which by looking. A structural assessment by an engineer is the first thing we arrange. If the bones are good, renovation is usually the smarter route. If they aren't, the conversation changes quickly.

2. Does the layout work for how you live now?

Many older homes were planned around separate, closed rooms. If you want open living, more natural light or an extra bedroom, the question becomes how many walls need to move — and whether they're load-bearing. Moving a few partitions is renovation. Re-planning the whole footprint starts to look like a rebuild.

3. What do the numbers say?

Renovation isn't automatically cheaper. Once you're replacing the roof, the wiring, the plumbing and the windows, costs climb fast, and hidden problems tend to appear once walls are opened. We compare both routes side by side — cost, timeline and the value you'll have at the end — before you commit.

Sometimes the most sustainable building is the one that's already standing.

4. What will the approvals allow?

A rebuild may need fresh planning approval under today's rules, which can change what you're allowed to build — sometimes for the better, sometimes not. A renovation within the existing envelope can be simpler. It's worth knowing which way the regulations cut before you decide.

5. How long can you be out of the space?

Renovations can often be phased so you keep living or working in part of the building. A rebuild almost always means moving out. For a family home or a business that can't close, that can be the deciding factor.
