Minimalism has a reputation for being cold — white walls, hard floors, nowhere to put anything. That isn't the kind we practise. The spaces we design are calm, but they are meant to be lived in: warm underfoot, generous with storage, and rooted in where they are.

Start with light and air, not objects

In a tropical climate, calm begins with comfort. Before we choose a single piece of furniture we look at where the light falls, how air moves through the rooms and where the heat builds up in the afternoon. Deep window reveals, cross-ventilation and shaded openings do more for the feeling of a room than any accessory.

Choose warm, honest materials

A minimalist room with the wrong materials feels like a showroom. The right ones make it feel like a home. We lean on textures that age well and belong here:

  • Timber for doors, shelving and joinery that brings warmth to plain walls
  • Limewash and textured plaster that soften light instead of bouncing it
  • Terrazzo and stone that stay cool underfoot
  • Woven raffia, cane and hand-dyed adire that add pattern without clutter
Fewer things, chosen well, with room around them to breathe.

Hide the everyday, show the meaningful

Clutter-free living is really a storage problem. We build storage into the architecture — full-height cupboards, recessed niches, benches that open — so the things you use every day have a place to disappear to. That leaves the surfaces free for the few pieces that matter: a family photograph, a piece of art, something you brought home from a trip.

Let the shape of the room do the work

Curves are a quiet way to make a space feel softer. An arched doorway, a rounded niche or a curved sofa changes how a room feels without adding a single object. You'll see arches throughout our work for exactly this reason: they frame, they soften, and they draw the eye without asking for attention.

Celebrate heritage, gently

A calm home can still be unmistakably Nigerian. One strong piece of local craft — a carved stool, a textile, a clay vessel — carries more meaning in a quiet room than a wall of decorative objects ever could.
