Most building projects in Lagos don't go wrong on site. They go wrong months earlier — in the paperwork that wasn't checked, the soil that wasn't tested, or the budget that assumed nothing would change. Here is what we sort out with every client before the first block is laid.

1. Make sure the land is really yours to build on

Before design starts, confirm the title. That usually means a Certificate of Occupancy or a registered Governor's Consent, plus a survey plan that matches what you see on the ground. A lawyer should search the title at the land registry. It is slow and unglamorous, and it is the single most expensive thing to discover late.

2. Test the soil before you design the foundation

Large parts of Lagos sit on reclaimed or waterlogged ground. A soil investigation tells your structural engineer what the ground can carry and whether you need a raft, piles or something in between. Skipping it doesn't save money — it just moves the cost into cracks you'll be fixing for years.

3. Get approvals in parallel with design, not after it

Planning permits in Lagos State are issued by the Lagos State Physical Planning Permit Authority, and your drawings will need to be prepared and stamped by registered professionals. We design with the approval in mind from the first sketch — setbacks, heights and coverage — so the drawings you fall in love with are the drawings that get approved.

The most expensive decisions on a build are made before anyone picks up a trowel.

4. Budget for what you can't see

Clients naturally budget for what they can picture: finishes, fittings, furniture. The costs that surprise people are structural and invisible — foundations, drainage, electrical capacity, water storage and a reliable backup power plan. We recommend holding a contingency on top of the construction estimate and not spending it on upgrades until the structure is complete.

5. Keep design and construction under one roof

When the architect, the interior designer and the contractor are three different companies, every gap between them becomes a variation — and a delay. A single design-and-build team means one set of drawings, one programme and one person accountable for the result.

A short checklist before you start

  • Title documents searched and verified
  • Survey plan and soil investigation complete
  • Brief written down: how you live, what you need, what you want to feel
  • Realistic budget with a contingency set aside
  • One team responsible from drawings to handover
